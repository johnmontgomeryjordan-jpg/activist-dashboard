Purpose

A predictive screen that surfaces companies likely to attract an activist investor — before the activist builds a position — so FGS can get in the door early for new business. It ranks the S&P 1500 on the traits activists screen for, measured against each company's industry peers, using public data.

Who it's for

An internal new-business tool for the FGS team — a lead-generation and prep aid, not a client deliverable.

What it is not

  • It is not client-facing and not meant for distribution outside the firm.
  • It is not a replacement for FactSet, Diligent, Bloomberg or a dedicated activism database — it points you at names worth a closer look using those tools.
  • It is not investment or legal advice, and not a prediction that any campaign will actually happen — only a measure of how strongly a company fits the profile activists target.

Methodology

The universe

Roughly the S&P 1500, screened to companies above a ~$1B market cap. Delisted, deregistered or long-dormant names are dropped automatically and kept out of the peer benchmarks.

How the rating works

Each company is scored on traits measured relative to its industry peers (grouped by 2-digit SIC code) plus recent event accelerants. Each triggered signal contributes points weighted by how severe it is (how deep in the peer tail, how cheap, how far returns lag). The total becomes a 0–92 activist-target profile index, shown as a band: 75+ Severe 50–74 High 25–49 Elevated <25 Moderate It is not a probability of a campaign, and is capped below 100 so it never implies certainty.

The signals

Valuation gap — cheap vs peers (low price-to-book); cheap on EV/EBITDA vs peers.
Capital allocation — cash-heavy balance sheet; under-levered; goodwill-heavy (an acquisition-overpayment / impairment-risk proxy).
Operating performance — low operating margin vs peers; low return on assets; weak or shrinking revenue; bloated SG&A.
Shareholder returns — 1-year total return lagging the S&P; multi-year (3/5-year) total return lagging the S&P (a structural underperformer).
Governance red flags — classified/staggered board; poison pill; dual-class super-voting stock; CEO pay rising while the stock lags (pay-for-performance gap, from the proxy).
Peer benchmarking — trails the company's own self-selected compensation peer group (the peers it named in its proxy).
Shareholder support — weak say-on-pay vote at the last annual meeting.
Insider activity — a cluster of open-market insider selling (Form 4).
Event catalysts — recent CEO/exec departure, earnings miss, impairment, layoffs, leadership change, a negative activist headline, or a sharp stock drop on a leadership-change 8-K.

Every signal on a company's profile carries an evidence card with the underlying number, the SEC math behind it, the peer cutoff, the period, and a link to the source filing.

Active situations (already engaged)

When an activist has already shown up, the name moves off the proactive shortlist into Active Situations at one of three confidence tiers: Confirmed (an SEC 13D or contested-proxy filing), Reported (a known activist named in the press alongside the company), or Manual (tagged by a partner). These are tracked for awareness — they're past the point of a proactive pitch.

Where the data comes from

  • SEC XBRL — fundamentals: revenue, margins, SG&A, returns, cash, debt, goodwill, D&A.
  • SEC EDGAR — 8-K / 10-K / 10-Q events, Form 4 insider trades, 8-K Item 5.07 vote results, DEF 14A proxies (governance, CEO pay, peer group), and a 13D / contested-proxy sweep.
  • Finnhub — market cap, price-to-book, P/E, 1-yr return, earnings calendar, insider sentiment.
  • Twelve Data — daily price history and multi-year (3/5-yr) total returns.
  • Financial Modeling Prep — company profile and IR/contact details.
  • News API — activist / distress headlines, grouped on the Live feed.
  • Claude (Haiku) — re-voices the thesis prose, strictly limited to the facts above.

The AI thesis layer

The pitch thesis and talking points are first assembled deterministically from the computed numbers, then re-voiced by Claude Haiku for readability — constrained to only the facts we computed, never adding new claims. The deterministic evidence cards remain the source of truth, and a "✦ AI-polished" tag marks where the AI voice is shown.

How often it updates

  • Every 30 minutes — news, filings, market cap & valuation refresh, and scores recompute.
  • Daily at 6 AM ET — full SEC fundamentals, governance & CEO pay, insider, votes, the universe-wide activist sweep, multi-year TSR, price charts, the AI thesis, and the emailed pitch kit. The "lead of the day" rotates on this 6 AM build, so the site is fresh each morning and captures the prior night's filings and headlines.

Glossary

  • Activist investor — a shareholder who buys a stake to push for change (board seats, capital return, a sale, strategy shifts).
  • 13D — an SEC filing required when an investor crosses 5% ownership with intent to influence; the clearest signal an activist has engaged.
  • DEF 14A (proxy statement) — the annual filing with board, governance, executive pay, and the company's self-selected compensation peer group.
  • 8-K — a filing for material events (leadership changes, results, restructurings). Item 5.02 = executive/director changes; Item 5.07 = shareholder vote results.
  • 10-K / 10-Q — annual / quarterly financial reports.
  • Form 4 — a disclosure of insider (officer/director) open-market buys and sells.
  • XBRL — the structured, machine-readable tagging of figures in SEC financial filings.
  • Price-to-book (P/B) — share price vs. book value per share; low can signal undervaluation.
  • EV/EBITDA — enterprise value (market cap + debt − cash) vs. operating earnings; a core valuation multiple.
  • Goodwill — the premium paid over fair value in acquisitions; high goodwill flags an acquisitive history and impairment risk.
  • Operating margin — operating income ÷ revenue.
  • Return on assets (ROA) — net income ÷ total assets; how well assets generate profit.
  • SG&A — selling, general & administrative expense (overhead).
  • Total shareholder return (TSR) — stock return including dividends over a period.
  • Say-on-pay — the annual non-binding shareholder vote on executive compensation; low support signals discontent.
  • Classified / staggered board — directors elected in classes over multiple years, slowing a board takeover.
  • Poison pill — a shareholder-rights plan that makes accumulating a large stake costly.
  • Dual-class stock — share classes with unequal votes, concentrating control with insiders.
  • Compensation peer group — the companies a board picks to benchmark its executive pay.

Limitations & disclaimer

A few things to keep in mind so the tool is used well — none dramatic, just for awareness.

  • It can misflag. This is a screen: it will sometimes flag a name that isn't a realistic target (false positive) and miss one that is (false negative). Treat it as a starting point and verify before acting.
  • Free, non-commercial data tiers. The current feeds are free tiers, which means coverage gaps — e.g., the earnings calendar and say-on-pay parsing are partial, multi-year TSR and price charts cover the top-ranked names rather than the whole universe, and self-selected peer groups only resolve when enough peers are in our coverage.
  • Point-in-time figures. Fundamentals come from the latest quarterly filing; one-time charges can distort a single period's margins or returns.
  • Not advice. Nothing here is investment, legal, or financial advice, and the rating is not a probability that a campaign will occur.
  • Internal use only. This is an internal new-business tool — not for client distribution.

Roadmap & budget

Today this runs as a free-tier internal prototype for roughly $10 a month. That constraint — free, non-commercial data feeds — is what shapes most of its current limits. Below is what it costs to run now, what each capability looks like today versus with budget, and rough spending tiers to get there.

What it costs to run today

  • Hosting (Render Starter, always-on): ~$7 / month
  • Data (SEC, Finnhub, Twelve Data, FMP, News API): $0 — all free, non-commercial tiers
  • AI thesis (Claude Haiku, cached): ~$1–3 / month
  • Total: roughly $10 / month

The free data tiers are licensed for internal, non-commercial use — fine for a new-business prototype, but a client-facing or commercially-distributed version needs paid, commercially-licensed feeds.

  • Today — ~$10/mo. Free tiers, internal prototype.
  • Next steps — ~$75–150/mo. Paid Twelve Data + FMP tiers, a verified email domain, and a sturdier hosting plan → multi-year TSR/pricing across the whole tracked set, daily email to the full team, and fewer coverage gaps.

Figures are indicative planning estimates, not quotes — confirm current vendor pricing before committing. The single highest-leverage spend is the paid market-data tier, which removes the credit cap that drives most of today's limitations.
